Stars are so far away that, to us, they're essentially **points of light**. Their light crosses pockets of air at different temperatures and densities, each bending it slightly differently. The point dances and flickers: twinkling, or *scintillation*.

Planets are close enough to appear as tiny **discs**. Each part of the disc twinkles independently, and the flickers average out — so planets shine steadily.

That's also why observatories sit on high, dry mountains: less atmosphere, less turbulence, sharper images.
