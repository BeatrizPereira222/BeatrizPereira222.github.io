Right after the Big Bang the universe held almost only **hydrogen and helium**. Everything heavier was built later — inside stars.

## Stellar kitchens

Stars fuse light elements into heavier ones: hydrogen into helium, then carbon, oxygen and, in the most massive stars, all the way up to **iron**.

## Explosive delivery

When massive stars die as supernovae, and when neutron stars collide, they scatter these elements — and forge even heavier ones like gold — into space. New stars, planets and eventually people form from that enriched gas.

So the calcium in your bones and the iron in your blood were made in stars that lived and died before the Sun existed.
