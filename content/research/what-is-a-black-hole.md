Imagine throwing a ball upward. The faster you throw it, the higher it goes — and if you could throw it at about **11 km/s**, it would never come back down. That's Earth's *escape velocity*.

## When escape is impossible

Now squeeze a lot of mass into a tiny space. Escape velocity climbs. At some point it exceeds the speed of light, about 300 000 km/s. Nothing can travel faster than light, so nothing — not even light itself — can get out. That boundary is the **event horizon**, and what's inside is a black hole.

> A black hole isn't a thing so much as a place: a region of spacetime curved so steeply that every path leads inward.

## How big is it?

For a non-rotating black hole the horizon radius is the Schwarzschild radius:

```
R_s = 2GM / c²
```

Squeeze the Sun into a sphere about **3 km** across and it would become one. Earth would need to fit inside roughly **9 mm** — a marble.

## They don't suck

If the Sun were swapped for a black hole of the same mass, Earth's orbit wouldn't change at all. Gravity depends on mass and distance; black holes are only "dangerous" when you get close.
